# SafeTwin AI — Competition Abstract (Draft)

**Team:** Vistaraz  
**Selected focus area:** Digital Twin & Simulation  
**Impact domain:** Environment, Health & Safety (EHS)

## Abstract

Building evacuations are often planned using static floor maps and fixed exit assignments. During an emergency, hazards can make a route unsafe while uneven crowd movement creates queues at doors and stairwells. SafeTwin AI is a digital-twin concept for helping campus and building safety teams rehearse these situations before an incident.

The system represents a building floor as connected spaces, corridors and exits. A user selects an illustrative hazard scenario; the twin visualizes hazard zones and simulated occupants, then compares evacuation strategies using route length, hazard exposure and exit capacity. The dashboard surfaces clearance-time estimates, route bottlenecks and the assumptions behind each comparison. The intended outcome is a practical rehearsal and planning aid—not autonomous emergency command or a replacement for trained responders.

The initial scope focuses on a single, verified building floor and synthetic occupants. The hackathon implementation will prioritize an explainable routing model and a repeatable evaluation: compare a nearest-exit baseline with capacity-aware, hazard-aware routing across the same scenarios, using simulated clearance time, maximum exit queue, hazard exposure and the number of agents safely reaching an exit. Results will be reported as simulation measurements with their assumptions, not as guaranteed real-world improvements.

Future work may explore authorized, privacy-reviewed aggregate occupancy signals from existing sensors or CCTV. No camera feed is connected in the current prototype. Any deployment would require verified building data, privacy review, human oversight and operational validation with safety professionals.
